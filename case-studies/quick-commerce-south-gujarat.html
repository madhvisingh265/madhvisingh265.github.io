Market-entry memo · Sizing and recommendation

Should Zepto build out South Gujarat?

Quick-commerce apps are racing into smaller cities. I sized demand in four towns between Surat and Mumbai (Vapi, Valsad, Navsari and Bharuch) from the bottom up, to see whether Zepto should open more dark stores there.

Self-initiated, November 2026Public data onlyNot affiliated with Zepto or any rival

Recommendation: not yet

In the base case, the four towns together generate about 2,300 quick-commerce orders a day. A small-city dark store needs about 800 orders a day to break even. The 11 dark stores already there (including Zepto's one in Valsad) would need about 8,800 a day between them. The corridor already has more stores than demand.

At a realistic 20% share, Zepto would get about 460 orders a day across all four towns, which is less than one store's break-even. My recommendation is to hold, watch three triggers, and revisit in 12 months.

1. Why this question, why now

Quick commerce means apps like Blinkit, Zepto and Swiggy Instamart that deliver groceries in 10–20 minutes from small warehouses called dark stores. It is still a big-city business: in a Flipkart and Bain discussion, 85% of its order value was put at the top six metros. Redseer estimates that non-metro cities account for 60–70% of retail spending in India's top 100 cities but only a little over 15% of quick-commerce sales.

That gap is why every player is expanding. Flipkart Minutes says its customer base in tier-2 and smaller cities grew about 25 times in a year. Dark stores in smaller cities are also cheaper to run: brokerage Emkay estimates a tier-2 store breaks even at about 800 orders a day, against about 1,300 in a metro.

Zepto is in a different position. It ran 1,139 dark stores in March 2026 (about half of Blinkit's 2,243), and its IPO filing plans roughly 1,900 stores by FY30. It has also slowed expansion to improve its store economics. So each new store has to earn its place, which makes it a good test case for a small-city cluster.

Decision question. Should Zepto open dark stores across Vapi, Valsad, Navsari and Bharuch in the next 12 months?

Zepto's orders a day across the corridor, against what four stores (one per town) need to break even.

  • Base case: 460 orders a day for the whole corridor, or 0.57 of one store's break-even. No single town comes close.
  • High case (10% of households, 6 orders a month, 30% share): about 1,700 orders a day, enough for two stores, not four.
  • What it would take: for four Zepto stores to break even, about 19% of households would need to order monthly (at a 30% share and 6 orders a month). That's about three times the tier-2 average today.
  • The incumbents' problem: the 11 stores already open would need about 8,800 orders a day to break even, nearly four times base-case demand. Either these towns order far more than the tier-2 average, or several stores are running below break-even to hold ground.

5. Recommendation

Don't build out the corridor in the next 12 months. Keep the Valsad store, add none, and put the capital into denser cities where Zepto already operates (Surat and Vadodara are its nearest).

Revisit when any of these triggers fire

  1. Blinkit adds stores in Navsari or Valsad. That would signal the existing stores are full, which this model says they shouldn't be.
  2. Zepto's Valsad store passes 800 orders a day for two months in a row. That's direct proof that demand is above the tier-2 average.
  3. Store costs fall enough to bring break-even under about 500 orders a day, for example through smaller formats or shared delivery fleets.

What would change my mind

  • Vapi's real catchment is bigger than my estimate. The Vapi Municipal Corporation, formed in 2025 by merging nearby villages, estimates about 4.5 lakh residents, against my 2.5 lakh. Daman and Silvassa are next door. If those areas are reachable within 10–15 minutes, Vapi alone could support a store. This is the first thing I'd check on the ground.
  • Industrial demand. GIDC Vapi and the Bharuch–Ankleshwar belt have large factory workforces and company canteens. A business-to-business offer (office and canteen supplies) isn't in this model and could add volume.

6. Limits of this analysis

  • Population is an estimate for about 2021, scaled by an assumed growth factor, because the 2021 census was postponed.
  • Ordering share, frequency, order value and Zepto's share are assumptions, shown as ranges. The conclusion holds across the low and base cases. It weakens only in the high case.
  • Store counts come from one third-party tracker, whose totals and per-platform counts don't always match.
  • The 800-order break-even is a sector-wide estimate, not Zepto's own figure.
